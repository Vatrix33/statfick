# Major Update: Adventurers Guild Quest System Rework and Complete Guild Interface Redesign

I want to rework the existing Adventurers Guild quest system and redesign the guild interface to make it more immersive, interactive, and visually integrated into the game.

This update consists of two major parts:

1. A rework of the existing Adventurers Guild quest types, including their objectives, mechanics, rewards, and availability.
2. A complete visual and functional redesign of the Adventurers Guild interface, turning it into an interactive representation of the actual guild interior.

The goal is to make the guild feel like a physical place where adventurers gather, quests are posted, companions are recruited, and information can be obtained.

**Important:** Before implementing anything, examine the existing Adventurers Guild systems, quest generation, reputation mechanics, quest rewards, companion recruitment, artifact selection, class information system, regional progression, and existing UI architecture.

Preserve existing functionality wherever it is not explicitly being changed. Integrate the new mechanics into the existing systems rather than creating unnecessary parallel systems.

# PART 1: ADVENTURERS GUILD QUEST SYSTEM REWORK

## 1. General Quest System

Rework the existing Adventurers Guild quest categories according to the specifications below.

The guild should offer six standard quest types:

1. Hunt
2. Cull
3. Deliver
4. Search
5. Escort
6. Travel

Great Hunt quests should remain a separate, special category and retain their existing mechanics.

Each standard quest type should have a clearly distinguishable objective, gameplay mechanic, difficulty, and reward structure.

### Quest Availability and Refreshing

- Guild quests should refresh once per in-game day.
- The daily refresh should generate a new selection of available quests.
- The player should be able to accept quests from the available selection.
- Once accepted, a quest should remain active until completed, failed, or otherwise resolved according to its mechanics.
- Refreshing the available quests must not automatically remove or reset an already accepted quest.
- Quest progress must be saved correctly.
- The player should not be able to accept duplicate instances of the same quest if this conflicts with the existing quest system.
- Quest generation should take the player's progression and the region's level range into account.

Quests should primarily relate to regions and locations the player can reasonably access.

Avoid generating quests that require travelling to inaccessible areas or regions significantly above the player's intended progression.

The player must hunt and defeat a specific empowered version of an enemy normally found in a region.

## 3. Cull

<badge color="success">Second-highest standard quest rewards</badge>

Rework Cull quests into dedicated enemy-elimination quests.

### Objective
The player must defeat a specified number of a particular enemy type within a particular region.

### Quest Generation
When generating a Cull quest:
- Select an enemy that naturally spawns in the target region.
- Define a required number of enemies that must be defeated.
- Assign a specific area or encounter zone within that region.
- Display the target enemy, required kill count, and designated area in the quest description.

### Spawn Rate Modification
Once the quest has been accepted:
- Highlight the designated area on the world map.
- Increase the spawn rate of the specified enemy within that area.
- Keep the increased spawn rate active while the quest is active.
- Do not unnecessarily increase the spawn rate of unrelated enemies.
- Ensure that the increased spawn rate does not result in excessive enemy density or performance problems.

### Progress Tracking
Display the player's progress in the quest interface.

For example:

`Defeat Goblins: 7 / 15`

Requirements:
- Only kills of the designated enemy type count.
- Enemies killed outside the designated region or area should not count unless explicitly intended by the quest.
- Kills should be registered immediately.
- Progress must persist when leaving and re-entering the region.
- The increased spawn rate should end when the quest is completed or abandoned.

### Rewards
Cull quests should provide the second-highest gold and reputation rewards among standard quests.

They should be more rewarding than delivery and exploration-oriented quests because they require multiple combat encounters.

## 4. Deliver

<badge color="success">Third-highest standard quest rewards</badge>

Rework Deliver quests into inter-regional delivery missions.

### Objective
The player must travel to an Adventurers Guild located in an adjacent region and return to the original guild.

### Quest Generation
- Select an adjacent region that the player can access.
- Mark its Adventurers Guild on the world map.
- Clearly display the destination and return requirement.
- Generate the quest only when an appropriate adjacent region exists.

### Quest Progression
The quest should follow this sequence:

1. The player accepts the Deliver quest.
2. The destination Adventurers Guild is highlighted.
3. The player travels to the designated guild.
4. The player interacts with the destination guild counter.
5. The delivery is registered.
6. The player must return to the original guild.
7. Interacting with the original guild counter completes the quest.

Reaching the destination alone should not complete the entire quest.

The player must physically return to the originating guild.

### Rewards
Deliver quests should provide the third-highest gold and reputation rewards among standard quests.

The reward should reflect the additional travel distance and the requirement to visit two separate guild locations.

## 5. Search

<badge color="success">Fourth-highest standard quest rewards</badge>

Rework Search quests into exploration and discovery missions.

### Objective
The player must travel to a randomly selected location on the overworld and investigate it.

### Quest Generation
- Select a random valid location on the map.
- The location must be accessible to the player.
- Highlight the target location on the world map.
- Ensure that the location is not inside an inaccessible or inappropriate area.
- Avoid selecting locations that overlap important quest interactions or locations where searching would break other mechanics.

### Search Interaction
When the player reaches the highlighted location:
- A Search button should become available.
- The player must actively use the button to investigate the location.
- Simply entering the area should not complete the quest.

### Encounter Mechanic
Using the Search button should trigger an encounter.

This encounter must have a **50% reduction to the player's normal escape chance**.

For example, if the normal escape chance is 60%, the modified escape chance becomes 30%.

Requirements:
- The encounter should be generated using enemies appropriate to the location and its level range.
- The encounter should trigger specifically when using the Search interaction.
- The player must deal with the encounter before the quest can be completed.
- The quest should correctly register the successful investigation once the encounter has been resolved.

If the player is defeated, the existing defeat mechanics should apply.

### Rewards
Search quests should provide the fourth-highest gold and reputation rewards among standard quests.

The rewards should reflect the exploration requirement and the additional risk of the encounter.

## 6. Escort

<badge color="success">Fifth-highest standard quest rewards</badge>

Rework Escort quests into dangerous travel missions involving a bandit ambush.

### Objective
The player must travel to another town and return to the originating Adventurers Guild.

### Quest Generation
- Select an accessible town in another region or an appropriate nearby location.
- Highlight the destination on the map.
- Clearly display the destination and the required return journey.

### Travel Progression
The quest should work as follows:

1. The player accepts the Escort quest.
2. The destination town is highlighted.
3. The player travels towards the destination.
4. A potential bandit encounter is triggered during travel.
5. The player reaches the destination.
6. The player must return to the original guild.
7. Interacting with the original guild counter completes the quest.

### Bandit Encounter Mechanics

During the journey, the player should have a chance to encounter a group of bandits.

The encounter must follow these rules:

- There is an 80% chance that a bandit encounter occurs during the journey.
- There is a 10% chance that no bandit encounter occurs.
- There is a separate 10% chance that a second bandit encounter occurs during the journey.
- The bandit encounter has a 70% reduction to the normal escape chance.
- Bandit encounters must trigger even when the player is travelling on an existing road.

The probability distribution should be implemented as three mutually exclusive outcomes:

| Outcome | Probability |
|---|---:|
| One bandit encounter | 80% |
| No bandit encounter | 10% |
| Two bandit encounters | 10% |

Do not independently roll the chances in a way that creates unintended probabilities.

### Encounter Timing
The bandit encounter should occur naturally during the journey rather than immediately upon accepting the quest.

The player should have to deal with the encounter before completing the expedition.

For the two-encounter outcome, trigger the second encounter at a separate point during the journey.

### Escape Chance
The bandit encounter must have a 70% reduction to the normal escape chance.

For example, if the normal escape chance is 60%, the modified chance becomes 18%.

The bandits should be appropriate to the player's level and the region in which the encounter occurs.

### Rewards
Escort quests should provide the fifth-highest gold and reputation rewards among standard quests.

Their rewards should be lower than Search quests but higher than Travel quests.

## 9. Quest Reward Hierarchy

Establish a consistent reward structure across the quest system.

The intended reward hierarchy is:

| Quest type | Gold and reputation ranking |
|---|---:|
| Great Hunt | Special category – highest |
| Hunt | 1st |
| Cull | 2nd |
| Deliver | 3rd |
| Search | 4th |
| Escort | 5th |
| Travel | 6th |

### Reward Balancing
Rewards should take the following factors into account:
- Quest difficulty.
- Expected completion time.
- Travel distance.
- Enemy level.
- Combat risk.
- Number of required encounters.
- Player progression.
- Region level.

Gold and reputation should scale appropriately with the level of the region.

Avoid using the same fixed reward amounts for every region.

However, preserve the intended ranking regardless of region level.

For example, a high-level Travel quest should not become more rewarding than a lower-level Hunt quest simply because its destination is in a higher-level region.

The hierarchy refers to equivalent progression and difficulty conditions.

# PART 2: COMPLETE ADVENTURERS GUILD INTERFACE REDESIGN

## 10. Replace the Traditional Guild Menu with an Interactive Guild Interior

I want to completely redesign the visual presentation of the Adventurers Guild.

Instead of opening a conventional menu containing buttons and text options, the player should see an illustrated representation of the interior of the Adventurers Guild.

The illustration itself should serve as the main navigation interface.

Different objects, people, and areas within the picture should be clickable and open their corresponding menus.

### General Requirements
- Use a detailed illustration of the Adventurers Guild interior.
- The illustration should be the main focus of the interface.
- Interactive areas should be positioned logically within the image.
- The player should interact with actual objects and people in the scene.
- Existing guild functionality should be retained, but accessed through the new interface.
- Menus should appear as small pop-up windows or overlays without completely replacing the guild illustration.

The result should feel like interacting with a physical guild building.

### Important Visual Requirement
The guild illustration should differ depending on the type of settlement.

There must be two distinct guild interior variations:

**Castle Town Adventurers Guild:**
- A larger, more established guild.
- More spacious interior.
- More elaborate furniture and decorations.
- A larger bulletin board and counter.
- A more established tavern area.

**Smaller Regional Adventurers Guild:**
- A smaller and more modest interior.
- Simpler furniture and decorations.
- A more limited but functional layout.
- A more compact atmosphere.

Both versions must contain the same essential interactive functionality.

Do not simply use the same image with a different label. The illustrations should visibly reflect the difference between a major castle town guild and a smaller regional guild.

## 11. Interactive Bulletin Board – Quest Selection

The bulletin board should be the main interaction point for accepting guild quests.

### Interaction
When the player clicks on the bulletin board:
- Open a small pop-up menu.
- Display the currently available guild quests.
- Allow the player to inspect each quest.
- Display its objective, region, difficulty, and rewards.
- Allow the player to accept an available quest.

### Quest Information
Each quest entry should clearly communicate:
- Quest type.
- Quest name.
- Objective.
- Target location.
- Required enemy or kill count, where applicable.
- Gold reward.
- Reputation reward.
- Relevant special mechanics.

For example:
- Hunt: Target enemy and spawn zone.
- Cull: Enemy type, required kills, and highlighted area.
- Deliver: Destination guild and return requirement.
- Search: Search location.
- Escort: Destination town and possible bandit encounters.
- Travel: Destination city and return requirement.
- Great Hunt: Existing boss target and associated information.

### Active Quest Handling
- Clearly distinguish available quests from accepted quests.
- Prevent the player from accidentally accepting quests that conflict with existing active-quest limitations.
- Display active quest progress.
- Ensure that accepting a quest correctly activates its associated mechanics.

The bulletin board should feel like a physical quest board rather than a generic list.

## 12. Guild Counter – Quest Completion

The guild counter should become the primary interaction point for completing guild quests.

### Interaction
When the player clicks on the guild counter:
- Check whether the player has an active quest.
- Determine whether its completion requirements have been fulfilled.
- If the requirements are met, instantly complete the quest.
- Award the appropriate gold and reputation.
- Update the player's quest state.
- Display a reward notification.

### Important
Quest completion should happen through the guild counter rather than automatically when the player reaches the final destination.

This applies to quests that require returning to the original guild.

Examples:
- Deliver: Complete after returning from the destination guild.
- Escort: Complete after returning from the destination town.
- Travel: Complete after returning from the destination city.

For Hunt and Cull quests, the counter should recognize when the combat objective has been fulfilled.

For Search quests, it should recognize when the investigation has been successfully completed.

For Great Hunt quests, preserve the existing boss-defeat and completion requirements while integrating the counter as the appropriate guild interaction where applicable.

### Completion Feedback
When a quest is completed:
- Display a small reward notification or icon.
- Show the gold obtained.
- Show the reputation gained.
- Optionally show additional rewards.
- Use a short, polished animation.

The notification should be noticeable but should not obstruct the guild interface.

Prevent duplicate rewards if the player clicks the counter multiple times.

## 14. Tavern – Companion Recruitment

The tavern should occupy the opposite side of the guild illustration from the main guild counter and bulletin board.

Clicking on a group of adventurers sitting or standing in the tavern should open the companion recruitment window.

### Interaction
- The player clicks on the adventurers.
- A recruitment menu appears.
- Available companions are displayed.
- The player can inspect their stats, class, equipment, level, and recruitment cost.
- The player can recruit eligible companions by spending reputation.

### Recruitment Level
The level of available recruits must depend on the level of the region in which the Adventurers Guild is located.

Requirements:
- Higher-level regions should offer higher-level recruits.
- Lower-level regions should offer appropriately lower-level recruits.
- Recruit levels should remain within sensible progression boundaries.
- Avoid generating companions that are dramatically overpowered or underpowered for the player's progression.

### Recruitment Cost
The reputation cost must also scale with the region's level.

- Higher-level recruits should cost more reputation.
- Lower-level recruits should be cheaper.
- Costs should reflect the strength and usefulness of the recruit.
- Ensure that recruitment remains a meaningful investment.

### Starting Equipment
Every recruited companion should spawn with:

- Exactly one melee weapon.
- Exactly one random armor item.

The weapon should be appropriate for the companion's class where applicable.

The armor rarity should be determined by the level of the region.

Higher-level regions should have a greater chance of producing recruits with higher-rarity armor.

Avoid giving every companion identical equipment.

### Starting Stat and Class Points

This is an important requirement:

**Newly recruited companions should spawn with all their stat points and class points available to spend.**

This means:
- Do not automatically distribute their available stat points.
- Do not automatically spend their available class points.
- The player should be able to allocate these points manually.
- The points should be visible in the character management interface.
- The companion should retain all available points until the player spends them.

This allows players to customize newly recruited companions according to their preferred builds.

### Recruitment Limits and Compatibility
- Respect the existing party and companion recruitment limits.
- Prevent recruitment when the player does not have sufficient reputation.
- Prevent recruitment if the party is already full.
- Ensure that recruited companions are correctly registered in the party and save system.
- Prevent duplicate or broken companion entries.
